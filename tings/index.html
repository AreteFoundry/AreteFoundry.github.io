The Tings guide

Tings is a local-first planner for habits, tasks, and a week agenda that fits around your real life. New users start in minimal mode — a clean, simple surface — and can switch to the full regular mode whenever they want more. This guide covers every feature, screen, and setting — start at Getting started if you're new.

Get started in 2 minutes Guided tours in the app How the planner thinks Open the app

Use the search box above or the contents on the left to jump around. Throughout the guide you'll see screenshots of the app in action. Note: they were all captured in light mode — the frames adapt to dark mode, so they read fine on either theme.

One-line summary: Tings turns "I want to do X about Y times a week" into a today list and a packed week — without the anxiety of rigid due dates, and without the vagueness of endless to-do lists.

What is Tings?

Tings is a personal planner that lives entirely on your device. It blends three things that usually live in separate apps:

Habit tracker To-do list Week agenda / calendar

Each thing you want to track is a Ting. A Ting can be a habit (repeats on a rhythm), a task (a one-off job, optionally with a due date), or a fixed-time event (a hard appointment like "Dentist Tue 3pm"). All three share one list, one history, and one search.

The planner looks at everything together — your habits, your tasks, the time you actually have free this week, the places you'll be, even the weather — and produces a short today list plus a week agenda that slots work into the gaps that genuinely exist.

Screenshot: the home list — today's agenda plus your habits and tasks

Who it's for

Tings is for anyone who plans their days — however they like to plan. There's no one style you have to adopt:

  • People who love strict streaks — set flexibility to 0 with a fixed window and Tings behaves like a firm tracker: miss it and it's missed.
  • People who hate rigid streaks — rhythms and flexible targets roll forward instead of resetting, so real life never breaks your chain of effort.
  • People somewhere in between — one habit locked tight, another deliberately loose; you decide per habit, not per app.
  • People juggling habits, one-off tasks, and fixed events who don't want three separate apps.
  • Anyone who wants their planner to respect real life — sleep, meals, commuting, the gym being across town, rain in the forecast.
  • Privacy-conscious users: no account, and your list stays on the device. The few features that touch a service are opt-in and clearly marked.

Whatever your style, Tings adapts: flexible or rigid, minimal or feature-packed, one habit or a hundred. It covers every kind of recurring commitment and one-off job — if you can express "when" and "how often," Tings can plan it.

Minimal & regular mode

Tings has two surfaces on top of the same engine. Minimal mode is the default for new users: a clean, uncluttered view that makes the core loop — add, plan, log — feel effortless. Regular mode reveals the full surface: agenda times, activity dots, weather chips, filters, and the week-by-day view.

Both modes run the identical planner. Your rhythm, flexibility, priority, time windows, places, and travel are respected exactly the same — the modes only change how much is shown on screen, never what's due or where things land.

Minimal mode — where everyone starts

Designed so a brand-new user never feels lost. It keeps the essentials and tucks the rest away:

  • Cards show the essentials — emoji, name, a one-line status ("ready to start", "2d overdue"), and how often (or the due date for tasks). No agenda times, weather chips, activity dots, progress pills, or quick-action buttons.
  • Home is grouped simply — today / overdue / coming up. No day-by-day week sections, no filter & presence row, no busy-block or travel cards.
  • Detail is one scrolling page — rhythm, days, time windows, duration, and due date. The history and effort pages are folded away.
  • The calendar shows a clean month grid — without the insight panes and filters.
  • Swiping a card gives you what you need — keep (for samples) and remove. No pin, snooze, activity, or session timers cluttering the surface.
  • Day-header pills stay — the "N missed" and "N open" pills still appear, so you never silently lose a day's shape.

Regular mode — the full surface

Everything minimal mode hides, plus:

  • Week-by-day sections with open-time pills, busy-block cards, travel cards, and the "missed" pill.
  • Richer cards — agenda times (or a symbol), status percentages, two-week activity dots, order-link marks, early pills, weather chips, and quick action buttons.
  • Full swipe actions — pin, snooze, activity history, and focus session timers; plus drag-to-reorder and "doing now".
  • The five-page detail view — history, schedule, effort, identity, and actions.
  • A full calendar overview — insight panes, most-active lists, coming up / needs attention, and topic & place filters.
  • The filter & presence row on home — "Current Place" chip, place and topic filter chips, and the filter sheet.

Which should you use?

Start here

New to Tings? Stay in minimal mode for the first few days. Add habits, log them, watch the status line guide you. Nothing is missing — the planner works fully underneath.

Stay minimal

If you just want a clean tracker with no extra chrome, you can use minimal mode forever. It's a complete app, not a demo.

Switch to regular

When you find yourself wanting more — the packed week, activity dots, snoozing, timers, filters — switch to regular mode. Your habits, logs, and plans carry over unchanged.

Switching modes

Tap the Tings logo (top-left) → settings → display → toggle minimal mode. It's instant and reversible — switch back any time.

Many screenshots in this guide show regular mode. In minimal mode the same features are there — just tidied away — and this guide marks what's hidden with notes like "hidden in minimal mode".

How it thinks

A few ideas shape every screen. Understanding them once makes the whole app click.

Rhythm over dates

You say "about 3× in 7 days," not "every Monday." Tings tracks whether you're on pace, not whether you broke a streak.

No streak guilt

Missing a day resets nothing. A behind card just means "do it soon" — the target rolls forward.

Capacity is real

The week has finite free hours. Tings knows your sleep, work, meals and travel, so the agenda only plans into time that actually exists.

Whole week at once

Tings plans the whole week together, not day-by-day. Something that doesn't fit today gets pulled forward to tomorrow, not dropped.

Local & private

Your list lives on your device. The few features that contact a service are opt-in, encrypted, and marked with a cloud-up icon. See Privacy & data.

Your style, your rules

Rigid streak-keeper or laid-back planner — Tings meets you where you are. Per habit, you choose the tightness: firm days and windows when you want discipline, flexible rhythms when life needs room.

One pipeline

Habits, tasks, and events share one list with common search, topics, places, and history — not three separate silos.

Getting started

From a blank install to a working today list in a couple of minutes.

  1. Open the app. A brand-new install shows an empty home with a hint. You're in minimal mode by default — that's intentional, and everything below works exactly the same in it. (See Minimal & regular mode.) On first run Tings offers a short tour: the guided start if the app is installed, or the platform-aware install guide in a browser first. You can skip it — nothing is locked.

  2. Try samples (optional but recommended). Tap the empty message, or tap the Tings logo → samples. Add one demo or all of them — timers, places, breakable tasks, prayer demos and more are in there. Sample items are tagged so you can remove them in one tap later. (See Sample habits.)

    Screenshot: the sample habits sheet

  3. Add your first Ting. Tap the + button (bottom nav or top bar). Name it, pick habit or task, set "how often," and tap add. The detail view opens on the schedule page so you can fine-tune. See The add sheet.

  4. Log it. On the home list, tap the pulsing icon (or double-tap the card) to log a completion. That's the core loop.

  5. Tell Tings about your week. When you're ready, open settings and set your busy times (sleep, work, meals) and places. The agenda immediately becomes smarter. Prefer the fuller surface? Turn off minimal mode under settings → display to unlock week-by-day sections, agenda times, and more.

You can use Tings forever without configuring busy times or places — it still tracks habits and tasks perfectly. Those settings only make the planner sharper.

Strict streak-keeper? Set a habit's flexibility to 0 for a firm, missable-as-missed habit. Want room to breathe? Give it a few flexible days. Every habit is adjustable — see Rhythm & flexibility.

How the planner works

The planner is what turns "I want to read 3× a week and exercise daily and call mom and do a 6h deep-work block" into a sane day. It's worth understanding the big picture before the details.

The short version

Each time you open the app (or change something), Tings builds the whole week at once in a background worker, then shows you today's slice. It places things in this strict priority order — top tier always beats the tier below:

  1. Hard rules win. Capacity, busy blocks, allowed time windows, pinned items — never broken.
  2. Maximise hours placed. Fill as many real minutes as possible across the week. If a can-wait item is squatting on a slot a daily block needs, it's peeled to another day.
  3. Minimise travel. Co-located errands share one trip; far clusters get grouped to cut commuting.
  4. Sooner & higher priority. When hours and travel are unaffected, do things sooner and favour higher priority.
  5. Preferences. Preferred time, place, weekday — and weather rules — are soft nudges. They never beat hours, travel, or urgency.

The inputs

Busy times

Recurring blocks to avoid — sleep, work, meals, commute. See Capacity & open hours.

Open hours

The budget left after busy times. This caps how many minutes of items a day can hold, not just clock time.

Fixed events

Timed tasks and appointments — placed by clock first, everything else works around them.

Habit fields

Duration, priority, flexibility, allowed/preferred windows, breakable + min chunk, places, days.

Places & travel

Your saved places plus travel edges between them (drive/walk/bike/transit).

Weather rules

Optional per-item forecast profiles that steer outdoor items toward better weather. See Weather guidance.

Order links

"Before/after" chains you set up — temporary for today, or recurring in Schedule. See Habit order.

Two engines: simple vs smarter packing

There are two packers, switched by the smarter packing toggle (Settings → advanced; on by default):

  • Off (simple) — a fast greedy pass. Instant; used for previews and as a fallback.
  • On (smarter) — an exact optimizer that solves each day globally, can hold multiple candidate start times per item and pick the best non-overlapping set, plus a week-level repair pass. Better on tight days where several items compete for one narrow gap, and it protects items that have very few open slots.

Both satisfy the same hard rules. If the optimizer can't load or a day's solve times out, Tings silently falls back to the simple packer so the agenda always renders. While the exact optimizer is still running you may briefly see a "fast preview" state in the day audit.

What happens when a day overflows

When capacity runs out, the weight function picks winners: a scarce narrow window beats a broad higher-priority item (the gym opens 6–7am; reading can happen anytime). Within the same scarcity band, higher priority wins (P0 before P5), and within a priority your home creation order is kept. Anything that can't fit today is pulled forward to another eligible day rather than dropped — only items with no viable day anywhere are left out. A habit whose hard window is genuinely too small for its duration is never force-fit.

Because the whole week is solved together, an item that doesn't fit today quietly lands tomorrow or later — you don't have to reschedule anything by hand.

Planner scenarios

The best way to understand the planner is to see what it does in real situations. Each scenario shows a setup and the resulting agenda.

1. Packed day drops the lowest priority
You set up
  • Cardio, 50 min, P0
  • Reading, 50 min, P5
  • Only 60 min of capacity today
The agenda
  • Cardio gets the slot
  • Reading is dropped today, rolled forward to a lighter day
  • The unused gap is flagged "budget-capped," not a scheduler miss
2. Errands across town share one trip
You set up
  • A morning home routine
  • Two errands at far-but-adjacent places (~28 km away, ~500 m apart)
  • Travel mode: walking
The agenda
  • Both errands land on the same day with a short hop between them
  • A thin travel card reads "leave by … · Nm · Home → FarA"
  • Total commuting is a fraction of two separate round-trips
3. A breakable task splits into chunks
You set up
  • "Write report," 90 min, breakable, min chunk 30
  • Day fragmented by blocks into a 40-min gap and a 50-min gap
The agenda
  • Splits into 40 + 50 (the largest valid piece per gap — never 30+30+30)
  • If forced into three 30-min gaps plus a 10-min gap, you get 30+30+30+10
  • The first card shows a progress crown; later chunks show trail dots
4. Habit chained after a morning routine
You set up
  • Three habits; you drag one to be right after "Morning routine"
  • An unrelated high-priority item also wants that slot
The agenda
  • The chained habit sits directly after the routine, nothing between them
  • The unrelated item is the one removed, not the linked pair
  • Order-link marks on the card show the partner's emoji
5. Prayer-anchored window beats a flexible P0
You set up
  • "Sunrise stretch," 5 min, allowed sunrise+5 → sunrise+35, P2
  • "Deep work," 60 min, anywhere, P0
  • Sleep until sunrise−8h
The agenda
  • The scarce sunrise window wins the morning gap for stretch
  • The flexible P0 deep work places outside that gap despite higher priority
  • A prayer whose window already closed rolls to tomorrow's window
6. Sleep block shifts with the sunrise
You set up
  • The sample sun-based sleep block (see Sample habits)
  • Your city is set
The agenda
  • Sleep always totals ~8h, wrapping overnight across seasons
  • The first open minute tracks sunrise — earlier in summer, later in winter
  • A sunrise habit between sleep and breakfast now places correctly
7. Plan-by defers to a lighter day
You set up
  • Work, 6h breakable, P0
  • A 1h clinic meeting tomorrow
  • Two plan-by errands (2h each) due later this week
The agenda
  • Work still hits 6h tomorrow
  • At most one errand lands tomorrow; both place on quieter days within their plan-by windows
  • If they'd fit the slack, both place ASAP — no over-deferring
8. "Do this now" jumps something to the top
You set up
  • Three equal-priority tasks on today
  • You long-press-drag one to the very top of today
The agenda
  • That task is pinned to the top and starts a session timer from now
  • It auto-logs after its duration, then clears
  • Completing any habit clears today's order edges touching it
9. Do-early pulls a flexible item into today
You set up
  • "Laundry," target 2× / 7d, flexibility 2 days
  • Its target day is overloaded; today has 4h free
The agenda
  • Laundry is pulled into today, shown under today with an early pill
  • If today were full, it'd fall back to "coming up" with no early pill
10. Open-time pill reveals the gaps
You set up
  • Busy blocks for sleep, work, dinner
  • A few habits with windows
The agenda
  • Each day header shows an "open time" pill with the free minutes
  • Tap it for a free/busy strip and a list of every open gap
  • No pill when the day has under 10 min free
11. Preferred time is a soft nudge
You set up
  • A habit with an allowed window all day and a preferred start of 2pm
The agenda
  • Placed at ~2pm when the session fits
  • But the nudge never overrides a hard allowed window or a busy block
  • If 2pm is blocked, it falls back to the clock
12. Weather steers an outdoor item
You set up
  • "Outdoor run" with a weather profile: prefer lower rain chance, hard max wind 35 km/h
  • Tomorrow morning is stormy; Thursday is clear
The agenda
  • The run's start time shifts toward the calmer, drier stretch of the day
  • Hard wind limit blocks flexible slots outside the bound — unless it's pinned, critical, or directly linked (then it plans anyway and shows a weather override chip)
  • If Thursday is clearly better, the run defers there

Capacity & open hours

The planner treats your week as a finite container of open minutes. Two things define it:

Busy times

Recurring blocks you don't want filled — sleep, work, meals, commute, school. Add them in Settings → busy times. Each has a start/end (fixed clock or dynamic), chosen weekdays, and optional place. You can override a single date without touching the recurring rule.

Open hours

What's left after busy times. The budget caps minutes of doable items per day, not raw clock time — so a 600-min budget means 600 min of items even if the clock has more literal gaps.

How free time shows up

  • Open-time pill on each day header — tap for a free/busy strip and the list of gaps ("room in your day"; tap a free block to plan into it).
  • Day audit — triple-tap a day header for a detailed scorecard (capacity, eligible work, the planner's decision trace). Power-user tool.
  • Cancelling a busy block for a date frees its exact minutes and writes a one-day override; undo restores it.

If a day is overfull, the lowest-priority items are dropped first and rolled forward — Tings never silently overbooks you.

Priority & urgency

Every Ting has a priority from P0 (critical) to P5 (someday). Priority claims today's time first, and is dropped first when a day overflows.

PriorityMeaningUse for
P0Critical — claims time firstNon-negotiables, deadlines
P1HighImportant recurring work
P2Normal (default)Most habits
P3LowNice-to-haves
P4LowerBackground goals
P5Someday — dropped firstBacklog, wishful items

Urgency is separate from priority. A low-priority habit that's been missed for days still surfaces, while a high-priority one that's comfortably ahead stays calm. The card's status color reflects the combination. Note: a scarce narrow time window beats a broad higher-priority item — the planner protects things that can only happen in one slot.

Priority also shows as a colored accent on the card left bar (P0 = strongest tone, P5 = muted).

Rhythm & flexibility

Two numbers define a habit's rhythm:

Target

times × in N days — e.g. 3× in 7d or 1× in 30d. The add and detail sheets pick the period in days.

Flexibility

How many days early a session can count. "2× in 7d, 2d flexible" lets Tuesday's session move to Sunday if the week is packed. On tasks, flexibility flips: how many days before the due date it starts surfacing.

Based on these, a card shows a status instead of a brittle streak (see Status). There's no streak counter and nothing "resets" — the rhythm just rolls forward.

Status: done / almost / behind

Every card carries a status driven by how recent your last log is versus your target rhythm:

done — on pace almost — close behind — needs attention new — not started yet
  • Build habits: "done" when last log is within target days; "almost" up to ~1.35× target; "behind" beyond that.
  • Limit habits: direction inverts — "done" when enough days have passed since the last log.
  • Stop habits: tracked by clean days; each clean day is a green dot.
  • New: never logged yet — surfaced gently so it doesn't nag.

The status pill on a card can show a percentage too (e.g. 72%), tinted to match. Toggle which marks appear in Look & feel.

Tings, explained

Every item in your list is a Ting. There are three families, each modelling a different kind of commitment.

Habits — build, limit, stop

Habits repeat on a rhythm. The add sheet creates a plain habit or a task; the kind is set (and changed any time) in the detail view's identity page:

build — keep it up limit — do less stop — quit entirely
build

"Exercise 3× in 7 days." You want this to happen often. Turns green when you're on pace.

limit

"Social media under 30 min/day." You're capping something. Logging still counts; the target frames it as a ceiling.

stop

"No snooze." A quit. Clean days (no slip) are good days; the history grid shows your streak of clean days.

Each habit has a target ("how often") like 2× in 7d, optional flexibility, a duration, and a priority. Stop habits deprioritise in the today list (they're absence-tracking, not something to schedule).

Tasks — one-off

A task happens once. No rhythm — just an optional due date. "Call the dentist back." Completion is simply your first log.

  • No due date → a "someday" task; sits in coming up until you plan or finish it.
  • Due date only → surfaces as it approaches (controlled by its flexibility window). A flexibility of 0 makes it a firm deadline.
  • Due date + time → becomes a fixed appointment on the agenda, exported to your phone calendar as a timed event.

Completed tasks aren't thrown away — they stay searchable (see Search & filters) and are cleaned up later by retention.

Fixed-time events

An event is a hard point in time — "Dentist Tue 3:00pm," "Flight 6am." Events are never rescored or shuffled; they're placed by clock and everything else is planned around them. They appear on the agenda and can be exported to your phone calendar. (You create one by giving a task a date and a time, or via calendar import.)

Emoji & visual identity

Tings leans on emoji to make your list scannable at a glance. A coloured circle with a familiar glyph is far faster to recognise than a wall of text — that's why the pulse icon on every card, the detail header, and the order-link marks all show the habit's emoji.

Auto-suggested from the name

When you type a name, Tings suggests an emoji after a short pause. The matcher tries, in order:

  1. Multi-word phrases — "walk the dog" → 🐕 (longest phrase wins).
  2. Single strong keywords — "run" → 🏃.
  3. Fuzzy matches — small typos still resolve.
  4. Modifier words (morning, daily, weekly) as a last resort.

The map covers ~300 concepts — fitness, food, faith (pray, quran, dua, ramadan, masjid…), study, work, parenting, home, sports, money, travel, screen time, and abstract goals (resolution → ⭐, quit/stop → 🚫, limit → ⛔).

Quick pick & your own

No good match? Tap the emoji preview tile to open the editor and either type any emoji or pick from neutral quick-pick chips (🔥 🌟 📌 🏆 ✨ …). These are deliberately chosen so a chip never fights an auto-suggestion.

Emoji background colours (disambiguation)

Every emoji sits on a coloured disk from a 12-colour palette (teal, amber, red, purple, blue, green, pink, orange, indigo, cyan, lime, slate) — or no background. Tings auto-disambiguates: if two habits share the same emoji, the second one is auto-assigned a different colour so the emoji + colour pair stays unique across your list.

🏃 teal🏃 amber🏃 purple no background

Default icon when blank

Leave the emoji blank and the card shows a tasteful default icon for the type (a heart-ish glyph for build, down-arrow for limit, etc.) in a muted tone.

Once you manually edit an emoji, auto-suggestion stops overriding it — your choice sticks. In the detail sheet, a habit that already had an emoji when you opened it is never auto-changed on rename.

Links & apps

Any Ting can carry up to four links — the people, apps, and pages that belong to it. Find them in the detail view's actions page under links & calls.

Kinds of link

phone WhatsApp FaceTime app link

Add an app in one tap

add app offers ready-made chips — Gmail, Outlook, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Reddit, LinkedIn, X — that open the service's web entry point; installed apps take over automatically. Anything else? Pick custom:

  • Paste an App Store share link and the app's real name fills in automatically (only the numeric app id inside the link is looked up — see Privacy). A hand-typed name is never overwritten.
  • Opens directly (optional) — paste the app's own scheme (e.g. spotify://) and Tings tries the app first at launch, falling back to the stored web page when it isn't installed. A hint in the editor explains how to find a scheme; a wrong one just opens the page.

Starred link & double-tap

Star one link to make it primary. From then on, double-tapping the item's card logs it and opens the starred link — "call mom" logs the call and dials in one gesture. All links also appear as buttons in the detail header.

Unsafe schemes are stripped, bare web addresses get https://, and store links written as itms-apps:// are converted to their normal web form — so links stay safe and openable everywhere.

Home: today's agenda + your list

Home is the screen you'll see most. It's today's agenda plus your full list of habits and tasks — not a dump of everything, but not just today either. What floats up: pinned items, things due or ready today, fixed appointments, and anything you've planned for soon. Everything else sits in coming up / the rest.

Regions, top to bottom

Top bar

The Tings wordmark + today's date (tap for About: tours, help & docs, samples, settings, privacy). On wide screens a search field sits in the same bar, with calendar / + / search buttons at its end. Hides on scroll-down, reappears on scroll-up.

Context row

Regular mode only: the Current Place presence chip, active place & topic filter chips, and a filters button. Hidden in minimal mode.

Reminder banner

Appears when reminders are on and a dated task or appointment needs attention. One consolidated row; dismiss for the rest of the day.

Quota bar

Only shows if storage gets large — warns that old dense activity may be compacted.

The list

Day section headers + habit/task cards + busy-block/travel cards. A loading skeleton shows on cold open until the planner supplies the week.

Bottom nav

Calendar · + (emphasised centre) · search. Search and calendar are hidden until you have enough to use them.

Reading a card

A card packs a lot into a small space. Every mark is optional (toggle in Look & feel).

Pulse icon (left)

The coloured circle with your emoji. Colour reflects status: teal (on track/fresh), amber (due soon), red (overdue/urgent), purple (a quit habit, recent), quiet (a never-logged task). Tap to log.

Name + agenda pill

The title (ellipsises first), and if it has a placed agenda row, a small time pill like "5:30p". "Scheduled time on items" in Look & feel switches this between show time, symbol only, and hide.

Status line (cue)

A short phrase: "Ready to start", "Planned today", "Clean today", "N days clean", "Snoozed for now", "Needs a day by today", "2d overdue", "Scheduled", "Someday"…

Context marks

Small icon pills: progress %, early, doing-now, order-link (arrow + partner emoji), sample, pin, due/scheduled/plan-by, rhythm, duration, flexibility, day-schedule, time-window, topics, place, next-plan, snoozed-until.

Weather chip

Logging & value logs

Three ways to log

  • Tap the pulse icon — quick log right now.
  • Double-tap the card body — same quick log — and if the item has a starred link, that opens too (log the call and dial, in one gesture). A single tap on the body opens detail instead.
  • Detail header pulse — logs from inside the detail view.

Taps are forgiving: if your finger drifts while scrolling, the tap is suppressed so you never log by accident.

Breakable cards log a chunk, not the whole budget

Tapping the pulse on a breakable habit logs a suggested chunk (the min, or the placed partial) — never the full remaining budget — so one tap can't accidentally "finish" a multi-hour task. Drag the crown to set a larger chunk; the pulse commits it.

Logging a value or note

If "log a value or note" is on (Effort page), each log can carry a number (weight, reps, pages, $) and an optional note. A small sheet asks for it; tap skip to log without a value.

Plans vs actual logs

You can also plan a session for a future/past day from the activity sheet or the history page. Plans are tentative; an actual log later consumes the plan. Toasts offer quick follow-ups like "done now", "plan instead", or "plan today" (see Toasts).

Swipe actions

Swipe a card horizontally to reveal quick actions. Swipe right reveals the left shelf; swipe left reveals the right shelf. Only one row is open at a time.

Swipe right → (left shelf)

pin / unpin keep (samples only) activity session / stop
  • Pin — keep above the auto-ordering (e.g. a morning anchor).
  • Keep — sample habits only: converts it into a permanent habit.
  • Activity — opens the per-habit activity and trend sheet.
  • Session / stop — start or stop a focus timer (timer-eligible habits only).

Swipe left → (right shelf)

snooze remove
  • Snooze — open the snooze sheet: 1d / 3d / 7d / 14d, or 1 / 2 repetitions.
  • Remove — delete, with an undo in the toast right after.

In minimal mode the shelves slim down to what a simple tracker needs: keep (samples) and remove. Pin, activity, snooze, and session timers appear once you switch to regular mode. The crown-dial area is excluded from swipe arming, so turning a breakable dial never swipes the card.

Reorder & doing now

Placed agenda rows can be reordered by hand for today. This is separate from the recurring Schedule links.

Long-press, then drag

  1. Long-press a card for a moment — it vibrates, a six-dot grip appears, and the card is armed.

  2. Drag it vertically. Drop indicator lines appear between rows.

  3. Drop between two cards to set an adjacency, or at the very top of today for "doing now".

The "Reorder?" sheet — temporary links

Dropping between cards opens "Reorder? · Just for today. Clears when done." For each neighbour you pick:

off

No link.

below / above

"Sometime" adjacency — comes after/before the neighbour, but other things can go between.

right below / right above

"Direct" adjacency — nothing may go between them.

These order links are today-only. Completing a habit clears today's edges touching it; completing a one-shot task also clears future-day edges. The Actions page lists every link with an unlink button and a clear-all.

"Do this now" — drop at the top of today

Dropping a card at the very top of today opens "Do this Now? · Keeps this on top and logs it automatically after Nm." Confirming:

  • Pins it to the top of today from this moment.
  • Starts a session timer that auto-logs the habit when its target time passes, then clears.
  • Optionally links the card below (right-above / sometime).

Doing-now is per calendar day and clears on day rollover. Manual mode (if you start a session by hand) never auto-expires — it runs until you stop it.

Order links never conflict with recurring Schedule links — if you try to set one that clashes, Tings tells you to edit Habit order in Schedule instead.

Easier reach

On big phones the top cards can be a stretch. Turn on easier reach (default on) and pull down and hold at the top — the first cards slide down into thumb range for a few seconds. A hard upward flick at the top triggers it too. The gesture auto-dismisses and any touch cancels the hold.

Search & filters

Search

Tap the search icon (or the search field on wide screens) to look up habits. Search is a lookup, not an agenda view — it skips day sections, busy/travel cards, and today/overdue grouping; results are matching items ranked by relevance (exact name first, then name prefix, then name contains, then emoji, then topics).

What it matches:

  • Name and emoji — plus kind keywords, so "quit" finds stop habits and "appointment" finds scheduled tasks.
  • Topics and place names & addresses.
  • Days — scheduled weekday and month-day words ("tuesday", "1st"), and task due dates.

Completed tasks stay searchable as an archive. The search button appears once you have ~10 items, or sooner if you have completed tasks. On a keyboard, typing funnels straight into the open search field; Esc clears the query first, then closes.

The add sheet

Tap + to add a Ting. The basics are on top; tap more options for the rest.

Top-level fields

Name

Up to 60 chars. An emoji is auto-suggested from the name; tap the preview tile to edit it (input, quick-pick chips, background colours). See Emoji.

habit or task. (The build/limit/stop kind is chosen later in the detail view — see Habits.)

How often

For habits: times × in N days. Help text adapts to the kind (build / limit / stop / task).

Due date & time

Tasks only. Date alone = soft deadline (flexibility 0 makes it firm). Date + time = a fixed appointment on the agenda and calendar export.

P0–P5. "P0 claims today's time first; P5 is dropped first when the day overflows."

History page

The first detail page combines this item's calendar and its stats in one place — two views, switched by a calendar | gaps segment:

Calendar view

  • Two-week strip — past 7 days · today · next 6, with prev/next arrows and a today snap. Each day shows up to four dots: done / almost / behind, planned, and a ring for on agenda (placed by the week planner). Schedule-eligible free days get a soft blue tint.

Schedule page

Tells the planner when a Ting is allowed and when it's best. Two layers: allowed (hard — "the planner only places this inside these days and hours") and preferred (soft). Toggle between them at the top.

  • Rhythm (habits) — the times × in N days fields.
  • Due (tasks) — date and optional time ("add a time to make this a fixed appointment").
  • Flexibility — days early it can count (habits) / days before due it surfaces (tasks).
  • Item order — persistent schedule links (before/after another habit, direct or sometime, optional same-day).
  • Days — weekday chips (sun–sat) and a "month days" disclosure for specific days of the month (1–31), summarised as "1–5, 15, 22–31".
  • Time window (allowed) / best time (preferred) — see Days & time windows and Dynamic times.
  • Places — chips for the places the window above applies to.
  • Weather guidance — attach a weather profile and, if the item happens far from home, a forecast place.
  • Specific times & places — per-occurrence alternatives. See below.

Specific times & places

Under Schedule → allowed there's an add option button. Each option row is a complete alternative for one occurrence of the habit — its own days, its own start/end window (fixed clock or relative to sunrise/prayers), its own place, and an optional preference (strong / soft / avoid) that overrides the place ranking for that row only.

"Days, hours, and places above apply everywhere. Add an option when one place needs its own time — for example 30 min after Sunrise at the mosque. Options are alternatives: the planner picks one per occurrence."

  • One occurrence, one option. If a general window and two rows all fit, the planner chooses one — it never schedules or counts the habit twice.
  • Rows can name extra places that are only valid inside that row's window; a place's own opening hours still apply as an outer constraint.
  • Both planners enumerate the general window and every row independently, travel included, then pick the best.
  • Many items only need the rows — leave the general window blank if the rows cover it.

Days & time windows

Days

Pick weekdays (sun–sat) and, behind the "month days" disclosure, specific days of the month (1–31). "Any" is the default.

Time windows — allowed vs preferred

Allowed (hard)

A starts–ends window the planner must respect. Once it closes, the habit leaves today's agenda and is marked overdue.

Preferred (soft)

A "best time" nudge. The day stays open and the habit still places late if needed — the nudge never overrides a busy block.

Two modes per endpoint

  • Fixed — a clock time like 06:00.
  • Dynamic — relative to an anchor: sunrise/sunset/prayer times, or even another habit's last log. Add a signed offset, combine two expressions, and see a live resolved preview.

Each endpoint has a gear toggle to switch between clock and relative modes, and a clear button. A live "resolved" line shows the current clock time the expression computes to.

Dynamic & prayer-anchored times

Dynamic windows are how Tings handles times that move — sunrise, sunset, prayer times, or "after my workout habit." Any start/end on a habit window or a busy time can be dynamic.

Anchors

sunrisesunset / maghrib fajr · dhuhr · asr · isha another habit clock time

Generic names are Dawn / Noon / Afternoon / Sunset / Night; turn on Islamic prayer names for Fajr / Dhuhr / Asr / Maghrib / Isha.

Building an expression

  1. Pick an anchor (prayer, sunrise/sunset, or a habit).

  2. Add an offset in minutes with a +/− sign toggle (e.g. "sunrise +30", "isha −15m", "sunrise −8h"). Clamped to ±720 min.

  3. Optionally toggle next day to use tomorrow's anchor (so "sunrise −8h, next day" means tonight's bedtime against tomorrow's sunrise).

  4. Optionally combine a second expression with whichever is later / whichever is earlier — e.g. "earlier of maghrib+1h · isha−15m". The second expression can be another anchor or a clock time.

A live resolved preview shows the clock time, or tells you what's missing ("add a location", "pick a habit", "never logged").

Habit-relative anchors

An anchor can be another habit's last log — "start 15 min after Gym ends." The window updates as that habit gets logged. Cycles (A after B after A) are detected and blocked on save. (Busy times support prayer/fixed anchors only, not habit anchors.)

Prayer anchors need a city or place with coordinates. "Set your city to use prayer times" if you haven't.

Habit order & links

There are two flavours of "this goes with that" — temporary and recurring.

Temporary order links (today only)

Created by dragging cards on home. "Just for today. Clears when done." Sometime or direct adjacency; cleared when the involved habits are completed.

Recurring schedule links

Defined in the Schedule page's item order block ("add link"). Each link:

Direction

before / after the chosen partner habit.

Adjacency

sometime (other things can go between) or right before/after (direct — nothing between; travel and fixed events can still appear).

Same-day

"Only on days with {partner}" — when on, this habit only plans with the partner (uses flexibility to join early, shown with the early indicator). Multiple same-day links are OR'd — it plans with whichever partner lands that day.

"Link this habit before or after any number of others. Links are OR'd: plan with whichever partner lands that day. 'Right before/after' keeps other movable cards out of the middle; required travel and fixed events can still sit between their times. Same-day pull uses flexibility so this habit can join a partner early (shown with the early indicator)."

Places & travel

A place is a real location with coordinates: Home, Office, Gym. Habits can be restricted to places, and the planner accounts for travel time between consecutive places on the agenda.

Adding places (map picker)

In Settings → locations, tap add place. Search an address, drag the map, drop the pin (or tap my location), or enter coordinates. Each place has a name, address, coordinates, a radius ("how close means you're here", default 75 m), and optional emoji. A place created from a new/edit habit form returns you there, already selected.

Per-place hours

Each place can have open hours and closed days — layered resolution: per-weekday override → closed days → default hours → 24h. A habit's effective window is the intersection of its own window and the place's resolved hours.

Live location (opt-in, private)

Enable live location so Tings knows which place you're currently at. Raw coordinates are never stored or sent — only the matched place id is kept. First enable shows a rationale sheet and requires a tap (an OS requirement).

City

A general area used for sunrise/sunset, prayer, and weather calculations when live location is off or a habit has no place. Set automatically from your first place, or type one (e.g. "Lahore"). Required for prayer-anchored windows, weather forecasts, and the sample sleep block.

Travel time

Travel between places is estimated (drive/walk/bike/transit), cached so it's never recomputed, and shown as a thin card between agenda items. Tap a travel card to override the minutes, open it in maps, or reset to the estimate. The default travel mode is set in locations settings.

Presence — "where are you?"

The Current Place chip sets today's starting place for travel math (it does not filter the list). Resolution: your manual pick → GPS geofence match → last known place → nearest place.

Home ranking stays location-blind — places only change the agenda order and insert travel cards, not what's "most important." A place used by any habit can't be removed until you clear the reference.

Weather guidance

Weather guidance makes the planner forecast-aware for outdoor items: you describe what "good weather" means for a habit, and the planner steers its start time — or its whole day — toward better conditions. It's opt-in, per item, and never nags.

Profiles

Settings → weather guidance. Create up to four named profiles (the first starts as "Outdoor"), then attach one to any item from the add sheet or the schedule page. Each profile holds up to eight rules about these metrics:

temperature (°C) feels like (°C) rain chance (%) precipitation (mm) snowfall (cm) wind (km/h) gusts (km/h) UV US AQI EU AQI

Rules

  • Prefer lower / prefer higher — steers when in the day the item lands (the planner compares the item's exact interval against the rest of the day).
  • Min / max bounds — absolute limits. Outside them is a bad slot.
  • Hard — rejects flexible times outside the bounds outright. Active, pinned, critical, and directly-linked commitments still place, flagged with a weather override chip.
  • Rules within a profile combine (AND). A rule with no preference and no bounds stays inactive (the editor says so). Each metric shows its scale and typical bands as placeholders.
  • Missing data always fails open — no forecast never blocks planning.

What the forecast covers

  • Forecasts come from Open-Meteo (no account, no API key) for your home city; air quality from CAMS ENSEMBLE when a profile uses AQI.
  • A 7-day hourly forecast refreshes about every six hours; within 90 minutes of a weather-linked item starting, a sharper 15-minute near-term refresh kicks in while the app is visible.
  • An item that happens far from home can pick a forecast place from your saved places (capped at four far places besides home).
  • The settings section shows a forecast transparency panel — the exact rows the planner reads, with fetch ages — plus a refresh forecast button.
Screenshot: the weather guidance settings with a profile

On cards, the weather chip is quiet by design: teal sun = good, amber rain cloud = caution or blocked, red shield = planned anyway despite a hard rule, gray cloud = unknown. Tap it for a one-line reason like "weather caution · wind 24km/h".

Attribution (shown in settings): "Forecast data © Open-Meteo, adapted for scheduling (CC BY 4.0). Air quality: CAMS ENSEMBLE via Open-Meteo." Only city/place coordinates are sent to fetch forecasts — see Privacy.

Calendar PDF import

You can import a calendar PDF (Settings → calendar import). Tings reads meetings from an Outlook/Teams agenda print or a Google Calendar schedule print and adds them as timed items already marked done on today's list.

  1. Choose PDF — Tings extracts events and shows a count ("N event(s) ready").

  2. Preview — every parsed event as a checkbox row with a live summary ("N of M selected · X timed · Y all-day"). Select all / none, deselect individually.

  3. Import meetings — only checked rows are added. Status reports what happened.

Credit habit

"Count meeting time toward" a build/limit habit (like Work). Meeting minutes merge per day (overlaps union, not double-count); the habit is auto-flipped to breakable if it isn't already.

All-day events

skip them (default) or add as tasks with a date. Dated tasks never count toward Work hours.

Re-import

Updates existing rows by a stable id — unless you renamed a local copy, in which case your edit is preserved.

Clear imported

Removes every imported meeting and strips calendar-credit logs.

For a one-off export the other way, use a task's export to calendar action to push a single item to your phone calendar as an .ics.

Prayer times

Tings computes the six daily times (Fajr, Sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) from your city/place and uses them as dynamic anchors for time windows — so a habit tied to "Fajr +20m" moves correctly all year.

  • Islamic prayer names — show Fajr/Dhuhr/Asr/Maghrib/Isha vs Dawn/Noon/Afternoon/Sunset/Night.
  • Calculation method — North America (ISNA) by default; only change if your mosque uses a specific method (MWL, Egyptian, Karachi, Umm al-Qura, Dubai, Moonsighting, Kuwait, Qatar, Singapore, Tehran, Turkey, Other).
  • Asr school — Shafi (standard) or Hanafi (later Asr). Affects only Asr.
  • Sample prayers — the samples sheet can add Fajr–Isha demos with ready-made windows.

No faith required — the same solar anchors (sunrise, sunset, dawn) work for secular routines like "walk at sunrise."

Shared display — fridge & tablet

Publish a limited, read-mostly view of today and tomorrow to a fridge screen or tablet — a family "what's happening" board that updates itself. Settings → shared display → create display feed.

Pairing (QR-only, secure)

  1. On the display device, open lanbeee.github.io/tings/agenda-display.html. It shows a QR and an 8-digit code.

  2. On your phone: settings → shared display → scan display QR, and scan from inside Tings — not with the phone's Camera app (a link opened by the Camera app is refused, on purpose).

  3. Type the 8-digit code from the display and approve. Pairing requests are single-use and expire in 30 seconds; five wrong codes destroy the request.

That's it — the display polls for fresh, end-to-end encrypted snapshots. Authorization expires after your chosen display reauthorization period (30 or 7 days); the display then shows a fresh QR to scan again. There is no silent refresh and no fallback link.

What you control

Feed title

The header the display shows (default "Shared display").

Publish scope

Next activities (1–50 rows) or hours ahead (1–48h) — never beyond tomorrow, 50 rows max.

Publish now / pause

Push a fresh snapshot, or pause publishing (marking done is disabled while paused).

Revoke

Kills the feed and every enrolled display; offline displays erase their cached copy when they reconnect.

Per item

Each Ting's actions page decides its display behaviour: mark done (default), view only, or hidden.

Screenshot: the shared display settings

On the display

  • Day sections for today and tomorrow — items with place and start time, busy blocks, travel rows, and open-time rows; the current item is highlighted.
  • Mark done — completable items show a tap target. Tapping marks it done instantly with an undo toast; the completion syncs back to your phone after the undo window and becomes a real log. Breakable habits credit partial minutes.
  • Night clock — swipe left (or "hide agenda") for a full-screen clock; three taps bring the agenda back.
  • ⋯ display settings — full screen, light/dark/system theme (dark default), text size (70–200%), and "screen fit" squash (85–100%) for stretched frames. Everything persists per display.
  • Honest banners — "paused", "plan is more than a day old", "reauthorization required", or "revoked" states are never hidden from the screen.

Private by construction: every snapshot and completion is encrypted on your phone (the key never leaves your devices) and the relay stores only ciphertext. Pairing is QR + code, and authorization always expires. See Privacy.

Share an item

Want a partner, parent, or friend to track the same habit — without sharing your whole list, an account, or each other's progress? Open the item's actions page → share item ("Someone else tracks their own progress").

  1. Tings packages the item's definition — name, emoji, rhythm, schedule, places, links — and encrypts it on your phone.

  2. It hands you an invitation link via the native share sheet ("Track this with me in Tings. Your progress stays separate."). The decryption key rides in the link, not on any server.

  3. The recipient opens the link in Tings; the item appears on their list and claims the invitation. From then on you each log independently.

  • Their progress stays theirs. Logs never sync back to you and vice versa — this is a template share, not live sync.
  • Revoke from the item when you're done; the invitation stops working.
  • The relay stores only ciphertext and hashed credentials — never item names, notes, or addresses (those live encrypted inside the bundle). See Privacy.

Backup, restore & cleanup

Your list lives only on this device. Export a backup regularly — and especially before clearing browser data or switching phones. There's no cloud copy.

Export / import

  1. Settings → backup → export backup. A file tings-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json downloads.

  2. Move it somewhere safe (cloud drive, email to yourself).

  3. On any device: Settings → backup → import backup, pick the file, confirm replace. A summary tells you exactly what will change before you confirm.

Invalid files are rejected with a clear reason and never ask you to confirm. Legacy bare-array exports are still accepted.

Cleanup / retention

Old completed tasks and extra habit history are trimmed about once a month when you open the app (you'll see a notice). Tune it under Settings → cleanup:

  • Completed tasks — delete after 2 / 3 / 7 days. Protected if still referenced (as a dynamic-time anchor, schedule-link anchor, or calendar credit habit).
  • Habit history — keep newest 12 / 30 / 60 entries per habit (or off). Always preserves plan logs; tasks aren't trimmed here.
  • Clean now — runs immediately, ignoring the monthly gate.

FAQ & troubleshooting

What's the difference between minimal and regular mode?

Both run the same planner and respect the same habits — they differ only in how much is shown on screen. Minimal mode (the default for new users) keeps cards, detail, and calendar clean and simple. Regular mode adds agenda times, activity dots, weather chips, swipe actions, week-by-day sections, filters, and the five-page detail view. Switch any time under settings → display. See Minimal & regular mode.

I want strict streaks — can Tings do that?

Yes. Give a habit flexibility 0 and, if you like, a fixed allowed window — it then behaves like a firm tracker: miss it and it's behind. Keep flexibility for anything you want to be forgiving. Every habit is tuned independently.

Where is my data stored?

In this browser/device's local storage. Clearing site data wipes it — export a backup first. The only things that ever leave the device are the opt-in, cloud-marked features listed under Privacy & data, all encrypted.

I lost my habits — can I get them back?

Only from a backup file. Without one there's no recovery (there's no server copy).

Why isn't my habit on today's list?

Check: is it snoozed? Hidden by a filter? Not actually due today (look in coming up in the overview)? Outside its allowed days/window? Lower priority than the day can fit? Has a same-day schedule link whose partner isn't eligible today? Blocked by a hard weather bound (look for the shield chip)?

Why does the agenda look empty / sparse?

The planner needs busy times and/or places to do its best work. Without them it still lists what's due, but won't show travel or open-hour calculations. Also check your home-page settings for which categories fill open time.

A habit shows "early" — what does that mean?

It has flexibility and was pulled forward into today because its usual day is packed and today has room. If today were full it would simply stay in "coming up."

The "missed" pill appeared — what happened?

Something was suggested for today but slipped off (its window closed, or the day overflowed). Tap the pill to see today's misses and anything still open from yesterday.

What does the weather chip mean?

Teal sun: conditions match your profile. Amber rain cloud: caution — a rule prefers elsewhere, or a bound blocks this slot. Red shield: it placed anyway despite a hard bound (it's pinned, critical, or directly linked). Gray cloud: no forecast. Tap the chip for the reason. Missing weather data never blocks planning.

Do reminders nag me about habits?

No — reminders cover dated tasks and fixed appointments only. Rhythm habits communicate through status colors and the missed pill instead. See Reminders.

Is the shared display / shared item private?

Yes. Both are opt-in and end-to-end encrypted: content is encrypted on your phone, and the relay only ever stores ciphertext and hashed credentials. The display pairs by QR + 8-digit code, re-authorizes on a schedule you choose, and can be revoked instantly. A shared item's key lives in the invitation link, and the recipient's progress never syncs back to you. See Shared display and Share an item.

Can I sync between devices?

Not automatically — Tings has no accounts. Use export/import to move a snapshot between devices. (The shared display and share item are one-way views/invitations, not sync.)

How do I start completely fresh?

Settings → start over resets settings only. To wipe habits too, clear site data (after exporting a backup).

My prayer-anchored window says "add a location"

Prayer/solar anchors need coordinates. Set your city (or give the habit a place) and the resolved time will appear.

The optimizer feels slow

Turn off smarter packing (Settings → advanced) for a faster, simpler pack. The simple packer satisfies the same hard rules; it just doesn't squeeze tight days as hard.
